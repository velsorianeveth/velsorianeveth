<p align="center">
some days, i feel like a dying lantern  
left burning in an empty house,  
where the roof has learned to crack  
beneath storms i never summoned.

the walls remember things  
i wish they could forget.  
a betrayal seeping through the wood,  
a familiar chair gathering dust,  
a voice that once warmed the room  
now folded into the silence of goodbye.

somewhere along the way,  
i misplaced the hands  
that once held mine through the dark.  
perhaps i was the storm this time,  
the careless wind that shook  
the branches i swore to protect.

my own mistakes became locked doors,  
and the names i used to call home  
grew distant beyond them.  
i keep revisiting those quiet hallways,  
searching for a moment  
where i could have chosen differently,  
where a softer word,  
a gentler heart,  
might have changed the ending.

i climbed a mountain  
with a sunrise stitched into my dreams,  
believing every aching step  
would lead me somewhere worth arriving.  
but the summit remained a distant star,  
and when i turned back,  
even the old roads felt unfamiliar.

around me, doors became quieter.  
footsteps wandered elsewhere.  
i stood outside a garden  
where my name once had a place,  
wondering when the gates had closed  
and why nobody heard me knocking.

so i stopped tending to my own little garden.  
the pages slept beneath the dust,  
the meals grew cold beside untouched cups,  
and sleep became a fogbound shore  
where the hours drifted without names.

i have watched the colors leave my days,  
watched meaning loosen from their branches  
like autumn leaves  
that no longer know where to fall.

i am tired.  
tired of carrying rain  
in a sky that never belonged to me,  
tired of being the keeper  
of a house already full of storms.

sometimes,  
i wish i could lay everything down,  
every stone, every unanswered prayer,  
every road i forced myself to walk.

perhaps the saddest thing  
about a fading lantern  
is not how little light remains,  
but how long it burned  
before anyone noticed  
the room had grown cold.
